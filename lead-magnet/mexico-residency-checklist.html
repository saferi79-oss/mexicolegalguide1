Mexico Residency & Visa Checklist

Your practical guide to navigating the Mexican immigration system

⚖️ Use this checklist before, during, and after your consulate appointment. Keep all documents organized in a folder — digital and physical.
Phase 1 — Pre-Application (at the Consulate)
1
Financial Solvency Check Do you meet the current monthly income or savings requirements for Temporary or Permanent Residency? As of 2024, minimum monthly income is ~$5,000 MXN (temp) or higher for permanent.
2
Passport Validity Ensure your passport has at least 6 months of remaining validity and at least 2 blank pages.
3
Application Form Download and complete the official consulate application form. Print single-sided only — some consulates reject double-sided printouts.
4
Passport Photos Specific requirements: star-eye distance, white background, no glasses, no smiling. Get 4-6 photos to be safe.
5
Proof of Income 6-12 months of bank statements, pay stubs, or investment statements. Must be stamped or have QR code if required by your specific consulate. Some consulates require apostilled documents.
Phase 2 — The Interview
6
Consulate Appointment Use the "MiConsulado" online booking system. Book early — appointments fill up 2-4 weeks in advance. Bring your printed confirmation.
7
Interview Preparation Be ready to explain why you want to live in Mexico. Do NOT say you plan to work locally on a tourist visa. Focus on lifestyle, retirement, or remote work. Dress professionally.
8
The Visa Sticker If approved, verify the 180-day validity of your visa sticker. Check that your name is spelled correctly before leaving the consulate.
Phase 3 — The "Canje" (In Mexico)
9
FMM / Entry Port Ensure your immigration officer marks your entry as "Canje" (30 days), not "Turismo" (180 days). This is one of the most common mistakes at the airport! Show them your visa sticker and say "Canje."
10
INM Appointment Complete the process at the National Institute of Migration (INM) within 30 days of arrival to get your physical Temporary Resident ID card (tarjeta). Bring: passport, visa, FMM, proof of address, fee payment receipt.
✨ Pro tip: Make a photocopy of every document before your appointment. INM offices often have limited hours in smaller cities — book early.
Quick Reference — Required Documents
Passport (valid 6+ months)
Application form (printed single-sided)
Passport photos (4-6 copies)
Bank statements (6-12 months)
Proof of income / employment letter
Consulate appointment confirmation
Fee payment receipt
Proof of address in Mexico (for INM)
