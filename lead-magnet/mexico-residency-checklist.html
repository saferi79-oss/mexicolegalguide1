The Ultimate Mexico Residency & Visa Checklist

Temporary & Permanent Residency — From Consulate Application to INM Canje
⚠️ IMPORTANT: Financial requirements for Mexican residency are updated annually (January) based on UMA. 2025 UMA: ~$113.14 MXN/day (~$3,442 MXN/month). Temporary Residency (2025): Minimum ~$46,000–$49,000 MXN/month income OR ~$570,000–$600,000 MXN in savings/investments. Permanent Residency: ~$172,000–$180,000 MXN/month income OR ~$2,300,000–$2,400,000 MXN in savings. Verify current figures at your consulate before applying.
1

Pre-Application — Document Preparation

4–8 weeks before appointment
1
Determine Your Visa Type Critical Temporary Residency (1-4 years, renewable) vs Permanent Residency (indefinite). Based on financial solvency, family ties, or job offer. Different requirements for each.
2
Calculate Financial Solvency Critical Verify you meet 2025 UMA-based requirements. Option A: Monthly income (last 6-12 months). Option B: Savings/investments (last 12 months). Option C: Combined. Use official INM calculator.
3
Passport Check Minimum 6 months validity REMAINING after visa issuance. At least 2 blank pages. If expiring soon, renew BEFORE applying — consulates won't issue visa into a short-validity passport.
4
Bank Statements — The Right Way Common Error Provide 6-12 months of statements. Must show consistent balance meeting requirements. Notarized/stamped by bank. Some consulates require apostilled documents. Digital printouts often rejected.
5
Proof of Income Documents Pay stubs, tax returns (last 2 years), employment letter with salary (Spanish translation may be required), pension/investment statements. Self-employed: tax returns + client contracts + bank statements.
6
Application Form (Formato Único) Download from your consulate's website. Fill online then print. Single-sided ONLY. Black ink. Don't sign until asked. Bring a blank copy as backup.
7
Passport Photos Specifications: 39mm wide x 49mm tall, white background, neutral expression, no glasses, visible ears. Get 6-8 copies. Some consulates are very strict — don't use a phone photo booth.
8
Proof of Address Utility bill, bank statement, or rental contract from YOUR COUNTRY of residence (not Mexico). Must match the consulate jurisdiction. Recent (last 3 months).
9
Criminal Record Check If Required Some consulates require apostilled federal background check from your country. This can take 4-8 weeks. Check with YOUR specific consulate BEFORE applying.
10
Additional Documents Checklist Birth certificate (apostilled if married/including dependents). Marriage certificate if applicable. Children's birth certificates. Passport copies of all dependents. Everything should be recent (less than 6 months old).
2

Consulate Appointment & Interview

The big day
11
Book Your Appointment Critical Use the "MiConsulado" online system (book at your assigned consulate based on your address). Appointments fill 2-4 weeks ahead — check daily for cancellations. Some open slots at midnight local time.
12
Pay Consular Fees Fees vary by consulate and visa type. Typically $30-$50 USD for application, plus visa issuance fee (~$300-$400 USD for temporary residency). Pay ONLINE before your appointment when possible. Bring receipt.
13
Prepare Your Narrative Common Error Be ready to explain: Why Mexico? (lifestyle, remote work, retirement, family). How you'll support yourself. DO NOT say you plan to work for a Mexican company without proper work authorization. DO mention remote work for foreign companies.
14
What to Bring to the Interview Original + 2 copies of EVERY document. Appointment confirmation printout. Fee payment receipt. Pen. Water. Phone fully charged (for digital documents). Folder organized by tabs.
15
The Interview Dress professionally. Be polite and concise. Answer ONLY what's asked. Don't volunteer extra information. Speak clearly. The officer has already reviewed your documents — they're verifying consistency, not testing you.
16
If Approved — Check Your Visa Sticker Verify: Name spelling matches passport exactly. Visa type (Temporal vs Permanente). 180-day validity window. BEFORE leaving the window, point out ANY errors.
3

Travel & Entry to Mexico

Within 180 days of visa issuance
17
Book Your Flight Must enter Mexico within 180 days of visa sticker date. Enter through ANY international airport or land border. Keep visa sticker intact — don't let anyone peel it off.
18
At Immigration — THE CRITICAL MOMENT Most Common Mistake Show your passport with visa sticker to the immigration officer. Say clearly: "CANJE" (not "turismo"). Ensure they stamp your FMM as "Canje — 30 días." If they stamp "Turismo — 180 días" your visa is effectively wasted and you'll need to leave and re-enter.
19
Verify Your FMM After immigration: Check that the FMM form says "Canje" and "30 días." Keep the FMM copy safe — you'll need it at INM. If they made a mistake, ask to speak with a supervisor before leaving the airport.
4

The Canje — INM Appointment (Within Mexico)

Within 30 days of arrival
20
Book INM Appointment Online Critical Use the INM appointment system (citas.inm.gob.mx). Select "Canje de canje/Canje de visa." Slots fill fast — book immediately after arriving. Choose an office convenient but not overloaded (smaller cities often have same-week availability).
21
Pay INM Fees Fee for Temporary Resident card (tarjeta) is approximately $6,000–$10,000 MXN (varies annually). Pay at the bank with the reference number provided in your appointment confirmation. Bring the original receipt.
22
INM Appointment — Documents to Bring • Passport (original + copy of all pages)
• Visa sticker copy
• FMM form (original)
• Fee payment receipt (original)
• Proof of address in Mexico (utility bill, lease, or hotel receipt with your name)
• Appointment confirmation printout
• 2 passport photos (same specs as before)
23
The INM Interview Brief and procedural. They'll take your photo and fingerprints. Your temporary resident card will be mailed to your address within 1-2 weeks, OR issued same-day in some offices. Verify all personal data on the card.
5

After You Get Your Card — Next Steps

First 30 days
24
Get Your CURP Your Clave Única de Registro de Población (CURP) is essential for everything: banking, SIM cards, contracts, tax ID. Get it online at gob.mx/curp with your resident card number, or at any RENAPO office.
25
Open a Mexican Bank Account You'll need your passport, resident card, CURP, and proof of address. BBVA, Banorte, and HSBC are foreigner-friendly. Some allow account opening with just passport + CURP + proof of address.
26
Register with SAT (Tax ID) Your RFC (Registro Federal de Contribuyentes) is needed for any business activity, invoicing, or tax compliance. Register as "residente temporal" with your CURP and resident card. Get a tax advisor — Mexican tax rules for residents are complex.
27
Healthcare Options As a temporary resident, you can register with IMSS (health insurance) voluntarily. Cost: ~$6000-8000 MXN/year (varies). Private international health insurance is also recommended and often required for residency renewal.
28
Renewal Calendar Important Temporary residency: valid 1 year initially. Renew before expiry. After 4 consecutive years, you can apply for Permanent Residency. Permanent = indefinite, no renewal needed. Set calendar reminders 3 months before each expiry.

Immigration policies change — always verify requirements with your consulate or an immigration attorney.
